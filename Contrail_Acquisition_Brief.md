## THE PROBLEM — AND WHY IT'S WORTH A BILLION DOLLARS

Every major industry that puts humans in contact with complex automated systems has eventually been forced to answer the same question: when the system fails, who is accountable, and how do we know what happened?

Aviation answered it in the 1970s. The Flight Data Recorder was mandated after crashes. Crew Resource Management was mandated after United 173. Both were resisted by the industry. Both became the reason commercial aviation is the safest form of transportation in human history.

AI is following the same arc — with one critical difference. The crashes are happening faster and at greater scale than aviation ever experienced, and the mandate is already written.

**The EU AI Act — August 2, 2026** — requires logging, transparency, and human oversight for all high-risk AI systems. It applies to any organization doing business in the EU. Fines for non-compliance: up to €30 million or 6% of global annual revenue. The US Executive Order on AI Safety has parallel requirements. The regulatory window is not coming. It is here.

The organizations that own the standard when the mandate hits own the market.
